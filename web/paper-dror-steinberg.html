Clever from the very first test

Dror & Steinberg

Dror, H. A., & Steinberg, D. M. (2008). "Sequential Experimental Designs for Generalized Linear Models." Journal of the American Statistical Association, 103(481), 288–298.

The problem

Neyer only switches on its smartest placement after it has gathered enough data. Could a method be that clever from the very start — and handle richer experiments than plain break/survive?

What it added

It applies the most-informative placement right from the opening test, so it's more efficient early on. It also stretches far beyond the original setting: testing in batches rather than one at a time, handling more than simple yes/no results, and tuning several dials at once (for example, two drug doses together rather than one).

The trade-off

It starts from a stated prior belief and updates it as results arrive. That makes it more powerful, but more complex to set up and run — you have to supply that starting belief.

Think of it as: the same smart searcher, but now expert from step one, able to search several rooms at once, and not limited to simple yes/no clues.
How it connects to your drop test: overkill for a single-dial drop test, but relevant if you ever varied two things at once (say drop height and object weight), or wanted maximum efficiency from the very first drop.
