"Search, estimate, approximate" — 3POD

Wu & Tian

Wu, C. F. J., & Tian, Y. (2014). "Three-Phase Optimal Design of Sensitivity Experiments." Journal of Statistical Planning and Inference, 149, 1–15.

The problem

Sometimes you don't want the overall picture — you want one far-extreme level, like where 99.9% of items break. That is exactly where Neyer is weakest.

What it added

A three-phase recipe — search, then estimate, then home in — purpose-built for those extreme levels. Tellingly, its middle phase is Neyer's clever placement step, reused inside a bigger plan. In head-to-head simulations it beat the earlier methods across a range of situations.

Think of it as: first roughly locating the building, then mapping it the Neyer way, then walking straight to the one specific door you care about — rather than mapping the whole place evenly.
How it connects to your drop test: this is the method to reach for if your real goal were a single tail number — e.g. "the height that breaks 99.9% of parts" — measured as tightly as possible. Neyer gives you that number (your all-fire), but with a wider ±; 3POD is built to sharpen exactly that. Good to name to your instructor as the natural next step.
