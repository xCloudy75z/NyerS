Reading the confidence ranges

Every number the tool reports comes with a confidence — an honest statement of how sure it is, given that you only tested a handful of items instead of millions. This page explains what those ranges mean and how to read the chart the tool draws.

What "confidence" means here

You never test enough items to know the true answer exactly, so the tool reports a range it's confident the true value lives in. "95% confident the average is between 49 and 55" means: if you repeated the whole experiment many times, about 95 out of 100 such ranges would contain the real average. The more items you test, the tighter the range. That's the whole trade — a few dozen items give you an honest, useful range without testing a million.

Two kinds of range, and why

NumberWhat its range looks likeWhy
Average, SpreadTwo-sided: "between X and Y"You want to know the plausible span of the true value on both sides.
All-fireOne-sided: "no higher than X"For "count on it working", you plan for the worst case — the highest the level might really be.
No-fireOne-sided: "no lower than X"For "count on it being safe", you plan for the lowest the safe level might really be.

The one-sided form is exactly how acceptance criteria are written — e.g. "fires at least 99.9% of the time, at 95% confidence." The tool matches that wording so your numbers line up with the spec.

In the printed report

The same information appears in words when you run the test:

AVERAGE breaking point: 52.00
  95% confident the true average is between 49.8 and 54.2.

ALL-FIRE level (99.9% of items break): 65.90
  Plan for it to be as high as 69.8 -- 95% confident the true
  all-fire level is no higher than that.
If a bound comes out odd — e.g. a no-fire bound below zero, or "could not be pinned down" — that's the tool being honest: with few items the range is very wide. The cure is always the same: test more items and every range tightens. You can change the confidence level itself in config/settings.m (confidence_level = 0.95).
