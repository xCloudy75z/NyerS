Tidying up both families — 3POD2.0 & Sen-Test

Wang, Tian & Wu

Wang, D., Tian, Y., & Wu, C. F. J. (2020). "Comprehensive comparisons of major sequential design procedures for sensitivity testing." Journal of Quality Technology, 52(2).

The problem

By now there were several rival methods and some rough edges. Which one actually wins, and can the leaders be polished further?

What it added

The most thorough bake-off yet, plus two upgrades: a refined Neyer-style method (Sen-Test) shown to beat the plain version, and a refined 3POD (3POD2.0). A key 3POD2.0 fix: it now gives the same answer no matter what units you measure in — earlier versions could shift if you switched, say, from millimetres to inches.

Think of it as: a referee's full ranking of all the searchers, plus tuned-up versions of the two front-runners — one of which no longer gets confused by a change of ruler.
How it connects to your drop test: reassuring context. It confirms Neyer-style designs remain competitive (Sen-Test even improves on them), and the "same answer regardless of units" fix is a nice point — your results in mm wouldn't change meaning if you reported them in inches.
