What this method does (in plain words)

Some things can only be tested by breaking them, and each test tells you only one thing: it broke or it didn't — never how close it came. This tool decides where to test next so each costly, one-use test teaches the most, and from the pattern of breaks and survives it works out two numbers:

  • the average breaking point, and
  • the spread — how much that point varies from one item to the next.

How it works — three stages

The tool keeps asking one question, "where should I test next?", in three stages:

1 · Find the zone. Start near your rough guess and reach further out, roughly doubling the step each time, until you've seen both a break and a survive. This works even if your guess is way off — it just costs a few extra tests.
2 · Close the gap. Narrow in between the highest survive and the lowest break, until the results start to overlap (a lower level breaks while a higher one survived). Until that happens, there isn't yet enough information for a real answer.
3 · Refine. Now a real answer exists. Each remaining test goes at the single most-informative level, sharpening the average and the spread. Stop when your items run out, and report the answer with its confidence.
The clever bit: the tool does not test at its best guess of the average. It tests slightly off to the side. Why? You're after two numbers. The dead-centre (even-odds) level is best for pinning the average, but tells you nothing about the spread — you can only see how fast "survives" turns into "breaks" by testing out on the slope. So it picks the one spot that is the best deal for learning both at once. That single idea is the whole method.
