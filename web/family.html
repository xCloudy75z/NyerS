Companion · the lineage

The family of sensitivity-testing methods

Neyer's 1994 method (the one you're using) has an ancestor it improved on and several descendants that improved on it. Here's the whole family in plain language — each with its own page. Click any title to go deep.

The thread through all of them

Every method keeps the same core trick — test where it teaches you the most — and each newer one fixes a single weakness: the slow start, the blind spot at the far extremes, or giving different answers when you change the units. It's a story of sharpening, not replacing.

1948 · ancestor

The staircase method (Bruceton)

Dixon & Mood — the "up-and-down" test

A no-computer rule: step up if it survived, down if it broke. Simple, but wastes items from a bad start and is weak on the spread.

1994 · the one you're using

The smart-placement method (Neyer, D-optimal)

Barry T. Neyer

Chooses each test at the most-informative level, self-corrects from a wild guess, and pins down both the average and the spread. The first to need a computer.

2008 · successor

Clever from the very first test

Dror & Steinberg

Applies the smartest placement from test one, in batches, with richer outcomes and several dials at once — at the cost of more setup.

2014 · successor

"Search, estimate, approximate" — 3POD

Wu & Tian

A three-phase recipe built for one far-extreme level (like 99.9%) — the exact place Neyer is weakest. Its middle phase is Neyer's step.

2014 · comparison

Neyer vs. 3POD, head to head

Ray, Roediger & Neyer

A careful side-by-side — notable because Neyer himself co-authored it, testing the newcomer's claims rather than taking them on faith.

2020 · successorcomparison

Tidying up both families — 3POD2.0 & Sen-Test

Wang, Tian & Wu

The most thorough bake-off yet, plus two upgrades: a refined Neyer (Sen-Test) and a 3POD2.0 that no longer changes its answer when you change the units.

2020 · free software

gonogo — a free R version

Paul Roediger

Runs the whole family (Neyer and 3POD2.0) in one place. Feed it your sequence and use its answer as an independent second opinion.

2024 · real lab

A real-world test, not just simulation

Journal of Energetic Materials

Ran the old staircase and the modern method on actual lab tests. The modern method wasted far fewer runs — a concrete saving of time and material.

ongoing · free software

senpy — a free Python version

Casey & Arthur

Neyer's method in Python — the closest free analogue to what you're building. A second opinion in a different language.
