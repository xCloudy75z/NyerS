A real-world test, not just simulation

A practical comparison of the old staircase vs. the modern method

"A practical comparative investigation of Bruceton and 3POD2.0 statistical testing methods on friction and impact sensitivity testing." Journal of Energetic Materials (2024).

Why it matters

Most comparisons live inside computer simulations. This one ran both methods on actual laboratory tests, so it shows the payoff in practice, not just on paper.

What it found

The modern method wasted far fewer test runs. With the old staircase, around half of one set of test series and a quarter of another had to be thrown out as unusable; with the modern method, almost none were — a concrete saving of time and material.

Think of it as: two recipes finally cooked in a real kitchen instead of described on paper — and one leaves far less in the bin.
How it connects to your drop test: this is your strongest practical argument. It's an impact sensitivity study — the same family as dropping objects onto parts — showing the modern approach wastes far fewer specimens than the naive staircase. With scarce 3D prints, "fewer wasted specimens" is exactly your win, evidenced in a real lab.
